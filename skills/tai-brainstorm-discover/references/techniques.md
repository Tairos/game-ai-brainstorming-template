# Techniques for branching sparks

Use these as prompts, not a checklist. The established methods below inspire our game-specific adaptations; the examples and “hardcorize/casualize” labels are local design prompts, not claims of standardized frameworks. Research is not required each time this skill runs unless factual comparisons or new research are requested.

## Established methods

### SCAMPER

[University of Toronto’s facilitation toolkit](https://cris.utoronto.ca/facilitation-toolkit/scamper/) describes Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, and Reverse. Apply selected transformations to a spark without demanding a solved design:

- **Substitute:** Replace an expected actor, material, resource, or sense. “A detective who interrogates buildings instead of people.”
- **Combine:** Connect two sparks, activities, or systems. “A rhythm game where your performance draws the dungeon.”
- **Adapt:** Borrow a relationship from another activity. “A courtroom run like a cooperative cooking game.”
- **Modify:** Exaggerate, shrink, or change a property. “A civilization that lives between two heartbeats.”
- **Put to another use:** Give something a different purpose. “Your inventory is the landscape other players explore.”
- **Eliminate:** Remove an assumption. “A racing game without steering.”
- **Reverse:** Invert a role, goal, or causal relationship. “A dungeon that is trying to escape its adventurers.”

### Random input / unexpected association

[Edward de Bono’s random-input teaching material](https://www.debono.com/teaching-creative-thinking-5) introduces an unrelated input to stimulate new connections. Pick a word or image, consider its properties, and connect one property to the spark. The connection can be evocative rather than mechanically resolved. Do not just attach an arbitrary noun as decoration.

For a gardening spark plus “echo”: “A garden that grows echoes of things that no longer exist.”

## Game-specific adaptations

- **Mix genres:** Let an action, rule, structure, or rhythm from one genre affect another. “A stealth game where hiding places are built by solving falling-block puzzles.” Genre labels alone can start a spark, but a surprising connection usually gives more to branch from.
- **Hardcorize a casual mechanic:** Introduce mastery, planning, interdependence, precision, or meaningful consequences into a familiar simple interaction. “A match-three board where every swap commits an order to a living army.” Hardcorizing need not mean faster timers, punishment, violence, or extra rules.
- **Casualize a hardcore mechanic:** Keep an appealing decision or fantasy while reducing execution demands, simultaneous responsibilities, or commitment. “A grand strategy empire governed through one postcard each morning.” Casualizing need not remove depth or merely change the art style.
- **Change agency or perspective:** Shift who acts, what is controlled, or what can be perceived. “You are the fog of war trying to keep two armies apart.”
- **Change time or scale:** Reframe duration, sequence, size, or frequency. “A city builder played during the final minute before demolition.”
- **Follow a feeling or contradiction:** Branch through atmosphere, impossible images, or incompatible desires without explaining the rules. “A homesick planet.”

Use casual/hardcore as shorthand for specific demands being changed, not fixed judgments about players. Techniques can combine, and none requires the branch to retain the root’s original purpose.
